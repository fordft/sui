# Product exploration

Expand the solution space before narrowing it. An audit finds omissions;
exploration finds better directions. Do both, in that order.

## Probe the outcome

- What is the user actually trying to accomplish — not what did they ask for?
- What work are we making the user do unnecessarily?
- Could we remove a step instead of adding another option?
- What would a first-time user misunderstand?
- What happens in the empty, slow, unavailable, or interrupted state?

## Consider real alternatives

For substantial design decisions, briefly consider two or three meaningfully
different approaches — including a simpler or subtractive one. Then challenge
the chosen direction:

- Which requirement does this satisfy? What realistic case breaks it?
- What is assumed rather than observed?
- Which existing behavior could regress?

Skip this ceremony for typo-scale tasks.

## Do not

- Do not add features because a checklist mentioned them.
- Do not prescribe "always add animations/onboarding/caching" — improvements
  are considered proactively, implemented selectively.
- Do not expand scope without justification; a better direction that doubles
  the work is a proposal to the user, not a silent upgrade.
